Liver filter arms: test results

15 November 2026, updated in the evening with where the false positives sit and the cc rule applied to the predictions, and late evening with the visibility (AUC) rule applied to the predictions, a sweep of stricter cuts and the tumour-size split; 16 November with the repeat ccAucRepair run (ccAucRepair_rep2) through every analysis · 7 nnU-Net runs trained only on MAISI synthetic CT, tested on 111 real LiTS cases · checkpoint_final

We generated 999 synthetic liver CTs with MAISI, cleaned them with two simple rules, and trained one nnU-Net per version of the cleaned set. This report shows what the test set says, why the filters probably work, and what that means for the next steps and the paper.

Main findings
  1. Every filter beats training on everything. Tumour Dice rises by +0.067 to +0.100. All 4 arms are clear wins (Holm p < 0.05, gain > 0.01), and the repeat ccAucRepair run confirms it (+0.079, p 6e−11). Liver Dice barely moves (−0.003 to −0.022).
  2. The gain comes from far fewer false positives, mostly outside the liver. The unfiltered model predicts 46 to 67 tumour pieces per case where there is no tumour, 84–88 % of them outside the predicted liver. The filtered models predict 9 to 12, mostly inside it. They still find about as many real lesions.
  3. It helps most where tumours are small. There, false positives dominate the Dice score. For large tumours the effect is small, and rejecting volumes even hurts.
  4. Repair beats reject. ccReject (343 volumes) and ccRepair (999) reach a similar mean, but ccReject misses more tumour, does worse on large tumours and overfits. Repairing labels keeps the data.
  5. Invisible tumour labels matter too, but less than the first run suggested. Adding the visibility (AUC) repair on top of the connectivity repair adds +0.028 in the first run (exploratory, p 0.004) but only +0.007 in the repeat run (p 0.54). With ccPost on both sides, both runs stay ahead of ccRepair (+0.032 and +0.015).
  6. A test-time cc rule does most of the same job. Applying the cc rule to the unfiltered model's predictions ("ccPost") lifts it by +0.078, as much as the cc filters. With both sides post-processed, ccReject, ccRepair and ccRepairDel no longer beat the baseline (−0.004 to +0.005).
  7. Only the visibility repair survives that, and a test-time version of it does the same job. ccAucRepair stays ahead under ccPost (+0.031 [+0.011, +0.051], Holm p 0.003); the repeat run by less (+0.014 [+0.003, +0.025], Holm p 0.09). Applying the AUC rule to the predictions as well ("ccAucPost", same cut 0.5475, readout fixed before running) lifts the baseline by +0.022. Against that, ccAucRepair with ccPost leads by +0.009 [−0.008, +0.026] (p 0.42), and the repeat run trails by −0.008 [−0.021, +0.004] (p 0.053). Averaged over both runs the lead is +0.000. With both sides given ccAucPost the first run still leads by +0.023 (Holm p 0.043), the repeat by +0.014 (Holm p 0.71).
  8. Run-to-run noise is larger than the two all runs suggested. The two ccAucRepair runs differ by −0.022 [−0.036, −0.008] (p 7e−5) as saved and −0.017 under ccPost, against +0.004 for the two all runs. The 0.01 noise floor is too small for arm-vs-arm comparisons. Every effect between filter arms (0.005–0.031) is of the size two identical runs can differ by.
  9. A stricter cut at test time helps more, up to a point. At 0.60 every run except ccReject gains another +0.02 to +0.04 (best 0.452, the repeat ccAucRepair run) for 2–9 real lesions lost per run. ccAucRepair's lead over the baseline is +0.008 (n.s.) and +0.015 (p 0.046) in the two runs, +0.012 (p 0.21) averaged. 0.70 gains nothing more and loses 13–33 lesions. The 20 val cases also favour ≈ 0.60. Exploratory: chosen after the results.

1What was compared

MAISI draws a full-body label map first, then the CT image from it. Two kinds of label errors are common in our pool (figures in reports/figures/filter_review/). Wrong-place tumours: 534 of 999 volumes have a tumour label ≥ 30 mm from the liver, often big blobs in the pelvis. Invisible tumours: tumour labels on tissue that looks exactly like its surroundings. LiTS labels only liver tumours, so both teach the model something wrong.

Each tumour component of each synthetic volume goes through the same two questions. The rules were fixed before any filtered model was trained (decisions of 12 and 14 Nov):

From these rules come four training sets ("arms") and the unfiltered baseline. All arms use the same nnU-Net setup, the same 20 real LiTS validation cases and the same 111 real LiTS test cases. Each arm changes one thing compared with the arm before it:

3The gain is fewer false positives

The filters change one thing above all: how much tumour the model invents. The unfiltered models put 46–67 tumour pieces per case where there is no tumour (35–41 ml per case). Filtered models put 9–12 (3–15 ml). Tumour precision rises from 0.55–0.57 to 0.70–0.85. Sensitivity and lesion recall stay within a few points of the baseline for the three repair arms. Only ccReject loses noticeably (sensitivity 0.46 vs 0.59–0.64, recall 0.35 vs 0.42–0.44).

4Where the false positives sit, and the cc rule at test time

For every predicted tumour piece we checked whether it lies inside the predicted main liver structure (the same cc rule as for the training labels, applied to the prediction: the piece of predicted liver ∪ tumour that holds the largest predicted liver piece). ccPost sets everything outside that structure to background. It needs no training and no extra data, so it is the obvious question a reviewer will ask: why not just post-process?

The unfiltered model fires all over the body. 59 and 39 of its 67 and 46 false-positive pieces per case lie outside the predicted liver. Most are specks (median 0.05 ml, 61 % below 0.1 ml), but they hold half of its false-positive volume. The filter arms have only 2–4 such pieces per case left. Their remaining false positives sit inside the liver and are larger (median 0.14–0.16 ml for the repair arms). The off-liver false positives are not random noise: the models paint tumour on round, low-contrast structures anywhere, the same places where MAISI drew wrong-place labels (lung, bowel, pelvis).

What this means The cc filters work mainly by teaching the model not to predict tumour outside the liver. A cc rule at test time does that just as well for the unfiltered model (+0.078) and costs only 2–10 lesions per run. So for wrong-place labels, filtering the training data and post-processing the output are interchangeable. Invisible labels inside the liver are different: their harm sits where ccPost cannot reach, and fixing them in the training data gives +0.031 on top of ccPost in the first run and +0.014 in the repeat (+0.022 averaged). Section 5 asks whether a test-time version of the AUC rule can reach them too.

Where ccPost goes wrong

ccPost trusts that the largest predicted liver piece is the liver and that the liver is one connected piece. When a model breaks down on a case, both can fail. Every run still improves its liver Dice under ccPost (+0.015 to +0.027), but each loses some real lesions.

What ccPost cannot reach

The false positives left after ccPost are mostly fluid or low-contrast structures touching the liver: a pleural effusion, fluid-filled bowel or gallbladder below the liver, a region at the liver's back edge near the vena cava. This is also why no run gets any of the 10 tumour-free test cases fully clean, with or without ccPost: their false positives lie inside the predicted liver. A connectivity rule cannot fix these. A check on the HU or shape of predicted tumour pieces might (not tested).

5The visibility rule at test time (ccAucPost)

If invisible training labels make the model paint tumour on normal-looking liver, those predicted pieces should themselves be invisible in the CT. ccAucPost tests that: first ccPost, then every predicted tumour piece inside the predicted liver whose separability is below 0.5475 (the training cut) is relabelled to its surroundings (liver if ≥ 50 % of its shell is predicted liver, else background). It uses the same code as the training labels, with the predicted liver in place of the label, and decides from the CT and the prediction only; the reference is read only for scoring. The cut and the main comparison were fixed in plans/PLAN_followups.md before the script ran.

Separability does tell false positives from real hits. Inside the predicted liver, real hits have a median sep of 0.89–0.90 and false-positive pieces 0.60–0.65 (AUROC 0.74–0.83 per run). At the training cut the rule removes 138–228 false-positive pieces per run (over 111 cases) and touches only 0–5 pieces that overlap a real lesion; 0–3 real lesions are lost per run.

What this means The test-time AUC rule recovers what training on the AUC-repaired labels gained: the remaining lead is +0.009 in the first run and −0.008 in the repeat, +0.000 averaged. So invisible labels, like wrong-place labels, are a problem a post-processing step can fix. With the same post-processing on both sides ccAucRepair stays ahead in both runs (+0.023, +0.014), but only the first is significant and the two runs differ by about as much as the lead itself. For the paper this weakens "only the training fix helps" to "the matching test-time rule does the same work; any extra gain from training is within run-to-run noise".

Stricter cuts

The cc rule already has no tolerance (a tumour piece must be 26-connected to the main liver), so "stricter" can only mean a higher visibility cut. A cut above 0.5475 no longer means "less visible than every real lesion": on the training side, 0.60 would call 5 of the 709 real test lesions invisible and relabel 338 synthetic components (instead of 162), 0.637 22 lesions and 479 components, 0.70 54 lesions and 715 components. So we tried the stricter cuts only on the predictions, as an exploratory dose-response check, and ran the same sweep on the 20 validation cases (the place a cut could legitimately be chosen).

  • 0.60 is the sweet spot on both sets. On test it lifts every run except ccReject by +0.033 to +0.062 over ccPost (Dice 0.434–0.452) and loses 2–9 lesions per run. On val the unfiltered runs also peak at 0.60–0.637, and every run drops at 0.70. A cut chosen on val would be ≈ 0.60, so the test result is not just tuned on test.
  • At 0.60 the training advantage is small and not consistent. Both sides with ccAucPost at 0.60: ccAucRepair 0.445 vs baseline 0.437, +0.008 [−0.009, +0.025], p 0.34; the repeat run 0.452, +0.015 [+0.003, +0.029], p 0.046; mean of both +0.012 [−0.001, +0.026], p 0.21. At 0.637 and 0.70 both runs lead by only +0.006. On val neither ccAucRepair run is ahead at any cut (20 cases, wide CIs).
  • 0.70 is too strict: no further gain on test, losses on val, and 13–33 real lesions lost per run.

6Biggest gain where tumours are small

With a small tumour, a few false-positive pieces outweigh the real lesion. In the smallest third the baseline's voxel precision is only 0.02. That is where the filters help most: Dice rises from 0.09 to 0.19–0.27. It is also where the two ccAucRepair runs differ most (0.27 vs 0.22; medium 0.43 vs 0.41, large 0.62 vs 0.63). With large tumours, false positives matter less (baseline precision 0.79), so the gain shrinks to +0.01 to +0.03. ccReject falls below the baseline there (0.53 vs 0.60): it under-segments big tumours.

Why ccReject loses on large tumours (likely) Rejecting a volume removes all of it, including its correctly placed liver tumours. Volumes with large or many tumour labels are more likely to have at least one bad component, so ccReject probably sees fewer large tumours. The trained model becomes conservative: highest precision of all (0.85), lowest sensitivity (0.46). The pool's tumour sizes per arm can be counted from the filter metrics to confirm this.

7Repair beats reject, though their means are close

ccReject's mean (+0.079) comes with the widest spread: 49 cases better, 31 worse, median gain 0.000. Its CI is twice as wide as the repair arms'. The repair arms move almost every case up a little (ccAucRepair: 66 better, 11 worse, median +0.054; its repeat run 65 better, 9 worse, median +0.038). Pooled over all voxels, ccReject barely beats the baseline (global Dice 0.594 vs 0.58–0.59). The repair arms reach 0.651–0.675.

What this means Reject and repair use the same criterion, so their difference is the action alone. Repair is the better default: same or higher Dice, fewer losers, better on large tumours, a smaller train/val gap (0.18–0.26 vs 0.35), and it keeps N = 999. The checkpoint_best predictions now running on UCloud will show how much of ccReject's weakness is overfitting (its best epoch was 775). Under ccPost the reject/repair difference in mean Dice disappears too (+0.005 vs −0.001), so the case for repair rests on the spread, large tumours and N, not on the mean.

9What did not get better

  • Tumour-free cases still get tumour. All seven models predict tumour in all 10 tumour-free test cases. Filtering cuts the amount (ccAucRepair: 11–23 pieces, 2–38 ml per case, vs 36–142 pieces for all), but not to zero. Neither ccPost nor ccAucPost helps here (0/10 clean in every run and variant): these false positives lie inside the predicted liver and are visible structures.
  • Lesion recall stays near 0.41 (0.75 for lesions ≥ 1 ml). Filtering removes wrong signal but adds no new signal. Small real lesions remain hard for a model trained only on synthetic data.
  • Liver Dice dips slightly in every arm (−0.003 to −0.022). The largest dips are in the arms with fewer volumes (ccRepairDel, ccReject). The same cases fail in every run (lits_043 ≈ 0, lits_013 0.04–0.43), so these failures are not caused by filtering.
  • Absolute level: the best arm reaches 0.377 tumour Dice on the 40 thesis test cases. The thesis model trained on real LiTS reached 0.653. Synthetic-only training is still far from real-data training. That was expected and is not what this study compares.

10How sure are we

Caveats
  • One training run per filter arm, two for ccAucRepair. The two all runs differ by 0.004, the two ccAucRepair runs by 0.022 (p 7e−5): run-to-run noise depends on the training set, and one pair understates it. The main claim (filter vs no filter, +0.067 to +0.100) stays above both. The arm-vs-arm differences (0.005–0.031) and ccAucRepair's leads under post-processing do not; ccAucRepair vs ccRepair, the one that looked solid, shrinks from +0.028 to +0.007 in the repeat.
  • Per-case mean Dice rewards removing false positives in small-tumour cases. That matches the clinical aim (don't invent lesions), but global Dice and lesion recall tell a more modest story for ccReject.
  • checkpoint_final only (fixed before results). The best-checkpoint test, a secondary check, is running.
  • Leakage: MAISI was trained on data that includes LiTS. This affects every arm equally, so it matters for absolute numbers, not for the comparison.
  • ccAucPost and the cut sweep were also added after the results. The main cut (0.5475) and the main readout were fixed in writing before auc_post.py ran. The stricter cuts are exploratory; val (20 cases) and test agree on ≈ 0.60, but a stricter cut would need to be fixed on val and confirmed on new data before it is claimed.
  • ccPost was added after the results. The main analysis (saved predictions) was fixed in advance. The ccPost comparison is exploratory, but it changes how the main result should be read, so it belongs in the paper.
  • CT examples are picked by fixed rules (largest case of each kind), not by eye, and read by eye. They illustrate patterns found in the numbers. Single cases do not prove them.
  • Size confound in ccReject: it has a third of the volumes, so "fewer but cleaner" and "fewer" are mixed. A random 343-volume control (backlog) would separate them.

11Implications and next steps

  1. For the paper, the story gets sharper and more modest. MAISI makes two kinds of label errors. Wrong-place tumours make the model fire outside the liver; filtering the training data fixes this (+0.07 to +0.10), and so does a cc rule at test time (+0.078). Invisible tumours inside the liver make the model fire on normal-looking liver; repairing those labels helps (+0.031 and +0.014 under ccPost in two runs), and the same visibility rule at test time gets that too (+0.022; remaining lead +0.009 and −0.008, n.s.). Report the main table, the ccPost table and the ccAucPost table side by side: the filters are an understanding of what goes wrong, and the matching test-time rules are the cheap fix.
  2. Recommended pipeline: ccAucRepair + ccAucPost at 0.5475 (tumour Dice 0.426 and 0.417 in two runs, cut fixed in advance; baseline + ccAucPost 0.403, ccRepair + ccAucPost 0.407). Most of the gain comes from the post-processing; the AUC-repaired training adds +0.014 to +0.023 on top, significant in one of the two runs. A stricter test-time cut (0.60: 0.445 / 0.452) is promising but exploratory.
  3. Run-to-run noise: before any arm-vs-arm claim in the paper (repair vs reject, AUC repair vs cc repair, training vs post-processing), each arm needs at least two runs; the noise floor should come from all repeat pairs, not from the all pair alone.
  4. Decide how to present the post-processing (open question in NOTES): main results stay as fixed in advance; ccPost and ccAucPost as clearly marked secondary analyses.
  5. Larger training tumours (decision Mon 16 Nov). The pool's tumours are not smaller per lesion (median 0.2 ml vs 0.3 ml in real val), but real cases carry more and bigger lesions (median 18 ml per case in val vs 2.3 ml per synthetic volume). Filter rules and post-processing

HU, W400/L40Hounsfield units, the CT density scale (water 0, air −1000). Images are shown with window 400 / level 40 (−160 to 240 HU).
Main liver structure (cc rule)Largest 26-connected liver piece; the main structure is the 26-connected piece of liver ∪ tumour that contains it. Tumour and liver outside it → background. On training labels this is the cc filter; on predictions it is ccPost.
Separability (sep)How visible a tumour component is in the CT. Core = the component eroded by one voxel; shell = voxels 2–10 mm outside it (no tumour), restricted to liver when the component sits in the liver. AUC = P(core HU > shell HU) + ½ P(equal), the Mann–Whitney statistic;[5] sep = max(AUC, 1 − AUC), so 0.5 = invisible and 1 = fully separated, for dark and bright lesions alike. Components with < 10 measurable voxels get no score (they pass).
AUC cutThe lowest sep of any real validation lesion (20 LiTS cases, lits_049 excluded as unreliable): 0.5475. A synthetic component below it is "less visible than every real lesion" → relabelled to its surroundings (liver if ≥ 50 % of its outer shell is liver, else background). On predictions: ccAucPost.
Contact shareFraction of a component's outer 26-neighbour shell that is liver.
AUROC (sep, FP vs hit)Probability that a random false-positive piece has lower sep than a random hit piece (+ ½ ties): the area under the ROC curve of sep as a detector of false positives.[6] 0.5 = no better than chance.

Comparisons and statistics

BaselinePer-case mean tumour Dice of the two identical all runs (all, all_rep2). Averaging avoids picking the luckier run on test data.
Paired differenceArm minus baseline per test case (same case, same reference), then averaged. Cases where either side is NaN are dropped.
Paired bootstrap 95 % CIThe 111 per-case differences resampled with replacement 10,000 times; the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the resampled means (percentile method).[7]
Wilcoxon signed-rank testTwo-sided paired test on the per-case differences: are positive and negative differences balanced around 0?[8] Gives the p values.
Holm correctionStep-down correction of the 4 main-arm p values for multiple comparisons: sort ascending, multiply the i-th smallest by (5 − i), keep the running maximum. The post-processing comparisons (sections 4–5) correct over 5: the 4 arms + the repeat ccAucRepair run.[9]
Noise floor, clear winThe two identical all runs differ by +0.004 in mean tumour Dice; any effect must exceed 0.01 (fixed before the repeat ccAucRepair run showed a 0.022 difference). A clear win = Holm p < 0.05 and mean gain > 0.01 (fixed 12 Nov). "Better / same / worse" per case uses the same ±0.01.

Training

nnU-Net 3d_fullresSelf-configuring U-Net framework;[10] default plans, 1000 epochs of 250 iterations, the same for every arm.
Validation lossnnU-Net's training loss (soft Dice + cross-entropy; negative because the Dice term is subtracted) on patches of the 20 real validation cases. Lower is better.
Pseudo DicennU-Net's running validation Dice, computed on sampled patches rather than whole volumes and smoothed with an exponential moving average; it tracks trends, not the final test Dice.
Train/val gapValidation minus training loss (centred 51-epoch mean); a growing gap means overfitting.
checkpoint_final / bestWeights after the last epoch (used for every result here, fixed in advance) / at the best smoothed pseudo Dice (secondary check, running).
